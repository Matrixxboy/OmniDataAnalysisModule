Probability Distributions

A professional guide to statistical distributions, their visual behaviors, and practical implementations in Microsoft Excel.

1. Normal Distribution (Gaussian Curve)

The normal distribution represents a symmetric, continuous probability distribution where most observations cluster around the central peak (the mean), and the probabilities for values further away taper off equally in both directions.

When to Use

  • When measuring continuous data (data that can take any value, including decimals).
  • When data clusters symmetrically around an average.
  • Common Use Cases: Heights, weights, exam scores, stock market returns, and measurement errors.
Density Curve with Standard Deviations

Excel Formulas & Implementation

Cumulative Probability (Most Commonly Used)

Use when you need the probability of a value being less than or equal to a specific threshold (e.g., finding the chance a student scores 85 or lower).

=NORM.DIST(x, mean, std_dev, TRUE)
Probability Between Two Values

Use when finding the percentage of data falling between two boundaries. Subtract the lower cumulative distribution from the higher one.

=NORM.DIST(high_value, mean, std, TRUE) - NORM.DIST(low_value, mean, std, TRUE)
Inverse Normal (Finding the Cut-off Value)

Use when you know the percentile (probability) and want to find the corresponding value (e.g., finding the exact score required to be in the top 10%).

=NORM.INV(probability, mean, std_dev)
Probability Density (Plotting the Curve)

Use the FALSE argument strictly when generating Y-values to draw the bell curve visually. It is rarely used for calculating actual probabilities in business.

=NORM.DIST(x, mean, std_dev, FALSE)

Real-World Application: Exam Scores

An exam has a historical average (mean) score of 70, with a standard deviation of 10.

What is the probability a student scores above 85?

Because Excel calculates probability from left to right (less than), we must subtract the result from 1 to get the "greater than" probability:

=1 - NORM.DIST(85, 70, 10, TRUE) ➔ 6.68%

2. Binomial Distribution

The binomial distribution models the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials, where each trial can result in just two possible outcomes (success or failure).

When to Use

  • When there is a fixed number of trials (n).
  • When each trial is binary (e.g., Pass/Fail, Yes/No, Defective/Functional).
  • When the probability of success (p) remains constant for every trial.
  • Common Use Cases: Quality control checks, coin flips, customer conversion on a fixed list of leads.
Probability Mass Function (n=10, p=0.5)

Excel Formulas & Implementation

Exact Probability

Use when you need the probability of achieving exactly k successes (no more, no less).

=BINOM.DIST(k, trials, probability_s, FALSE)
Cumulative Probability

Use when you need the probability of achieving at most k successes (k or fewer).

=BINOM.DIST(k, trials, probability_s, TRUE)

Real-World Application: Manufacturing Defect Rate

A manufacturing plant produces batches of products. The known historical defect rate is 5% (0.05). Quality assurance tests exactly 20 items per batch.

What is the probability that exactly 2 of the 20 items are defective?

We use FALSE because we are looking for exactly 2 defects, not 2 or fewer.

=BINOM.DIST(2, 20, 0.05, FALSE) ➔ 18.87%

3. Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution expresses the probability of a given number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space, assuming these events occur with a known constant mean rate and independently of the time since the last event.

When to Use

  • When measuring the count of events occurring over a fixed interval (time, area, volume).
  • When events are completely independent of each other.
  • Common Use Cases: Customer arrivals per hour at a store, network failures per day, number of emails received per minute.
Probability Mass Function (λ = 3)

Excel Formulas & Implementation

Exact Probability

Use when determining the probability of observing exactly k events in the specified time frame.

=POISSON.DIST(k, lambda, FALSE)
Cumulative Probability

Use when determining the probability of observing k or fewer events in the specified time frame.

=POISSON.DIST(k, lambda, TRUE)

Real-World Application: Customer Support Calls

A helpdesk typically receives an average of 5 calls per hour (this is our lambda, λ).

What is the probability of receiving exactly 3 calls in the next hour?

Because we want an exact number, we set the final argument to FALSE.

=POISSON.DIST(3, 5, FALSE) ➔ 14.04%
